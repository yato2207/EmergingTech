Closing reflection

LLMs and security vendors are both opaque technologies sold on promises a buyer cannot fully inspect. In both cases, trust followed verifiable evidence (pilots, proofs of concept, audits, measured ROI) rather than assurance. The scarce resource is warranted trust, and it is built by whoever makes verification cheap.

Self-reported gains can mislead

The respondent reported a 31–32% developer productivity gain. A randomised trial of experienced open-source developers found they took 19% longer with AI tools, though they believed they had been 20% faster (Becker et al., 2025). Populations differ, but the lesson stands: measure gains on real work before scaling.

Limitations

Case 1

One interview with a respondent who has a commercial stake in an alternative architecture, so it supports insight, not generalisation. Question 3 was covered only partly and Question 7 not at all. The 31–32% gain, $6–10 million fine-tuning cost and RBI liability point were not independently verified.

Case 2

Two interviews from different firms give a conceptual dyad, not a real one. Both sit in the Indian and Middle East data-security context. The refinery statistics are V1’s recollection, and the playbook is untested. Next steps: a CISO survey on the five roles, and a case study of one live vendor–client pair.

Ethics and consent

All interviews were online with adult professionals who joined voluntarily. Recordings and transcripts are used only for this course project. Case 2 participants were promised anonymity and appear only as V1 and C1.
